## Course Rules & Plan

### Textbook

Russell C. Hibbeler, *Engineering Mechanics: Dynamics in SI Units*, 14th edition,
Pearson.

Please purchase or prepare a printed copy. Printed textbooks and reference books
are allowed during exams.

### What is dynamics about?

- **Describing motion**: position, velocity, and acceleration (kinematics of a particle)
- **How forces determine motion**: the net force gives the acceleration (force & acceleration)
- **How energy converts**: potential and kinetic energy trade places (work & energy)
- **How rigid bodies move**: translation, fixed-axis rotation, and general plane
  motion; how forces and moments become angular acceleration

## Lab Assignment Guide

A self-directed-learning-week lab assignment, open to the whole class, to be
completed individually.

### How it works

| Situation | If you pass the assignment |
|---|---|
| Failing the semester | Semester total raised by up to 20 points (capped at 60) |
| Already passing | Semester total raised by up to 10 points (capped at 100) |

- This assignment is the activity for the "+2 self-directed-learning weeks" of
  the 16+2-week semester (12/28 – 1/8). If you are confident in your written-exam
  grades, it is entirely optional.
- Deadline: Sunday 1/11, 23:59. Late submissions are not accepted.
- Topic selection: students failing the midterm choose first; remaining topics
  are then opened to passing students. At most 3 students per topic.
- Sloppy work, fabricated data, or parameters that do not match your assigned
  value: fail — your original grade stands.

### Workflow (six steps)

1. Sign up for a topic in the LINE group (at most 3 per topic; pick another if full)
2. Prepare the equipment and film the experiment in a **single continuous take**
   (student ID card visible in the shot)
3. Collect data with Tracker or phyphox
4. Complete the measurements, calculations, and error discussion following the
   report template
5. Record a 2–3 minute **narrated video** of yourself (see the video rules)
6. Bundle the LaTeX source and data into an archive and email it to the
   instructor (see the submission rules)

### Personalized parameters

Every topic has an assigned parameter determined by the **last two digits of
your student ID** (call it N, N = 00–99), stated on the topic card.
Example: in the bouncing-ball topic, the release height is
h₀ = 100 cm + N cm; ID ending in 25 → N = 25 → h₀ = 125 cm.

The assigned parameter must be **visible** in the experiment video (measure it
on camera with a ruler). If your parameter does not match the assigned value,
you fail directly — this is the first plagiarism check.
